# MyEx — Design Reference Library

Researched 2026-11-24. Each entry carries two flags:
- **Kind:** REAL PRODUCT (shipping app) or CONCEPT (designer shot or case study). Paywall galleries, articles and datasets have their own labels.
- **Access:** FETCHED (page opened), SEARCH-ONLY (seen only in search results) or INACCESSIBLE (login wall, 403 or 404).

"Not verified" means the page text did not state it. Colours or fonts I could not confirm are never guessed.
Note that App Store listings show prices and ratings for one region only, captured on the fetch date.

## A. Breakup recovery apps

### 1. Breakup Buddy - No Contact
- **URL:** https://apps.apple.com/app/id1667956640
- **Company:** Applorium Ltd (fetched listing). A search snippet named a different developer, so treat the developer name as uncertain.
- **Flags:** REAL PRODUCT · FETCHED
- **Style:** Not verified. Release notes mention an "updated onboarding UI", an "improved journal UI", "profile color updates" and "improved cancellation screens".
- **Palette:** Not verified.
- **Type:** Not verified.
- **Patterns:**
  - 24/7 AI chat
  - CBT exercises
  - Journal with analytics
  - "Healing globe"
  - Social feed
  - Credit/token economy: 2,500 free chat credits, then Pro at $17.99 or $42.99 plus token top-ups at $7.99
- **Relevance:** The closest direct competitor to Mira: AI chat plus a journal, aimed at breakups.

- **Avoid:** The token system. Reviewers called it confusing and complained about AI memory limits. Use a simple subscription with no meters.

### 15. How We Feel
- **URL:** https://apps.apple.com/app/id1562706384
- **Company:** The How We Feel Project, Inc. (nonprofit). Product led by Ben Silbermann (Pinterest co-founder); science by Marc Brackett, Yale.
- **Flags:** REAL PRODUCT · FETCHED
- **Style:**
  - "Elegant color-coded matrix" of emotions
  - Reviewers call it "aesthetically pleasing"
  - A recent update added "liquid glass" design effects
- **Palette:** Colour-coded by emotion. The specific colours are not verified.
- **Type:** Not verified.
- **Patterns:**
  - Emotion-word check-in
  - Body-sensation tags
  - HealthKit correlations
  - One-minute video strategies in four themes
  - Friends sharing
  - A "write a negative story and watch it burn" activity
  - Free
- **Ratings:** 4.9 stars from about 30K ratings. Editors' Choice. Apple 2022 Cultural Impact award (per search snippet).
- **Relevance:** The best-in-class mood check-in, and it already ships Liquid Glass.
- **Adapt:**
  - Mood check-ins that pick an emotion word, not just a 1–5 score
  - A "burn it" ritual. It is close to Draft Jail and could be its release animation ("let it burn" instead of sending)
- **Avoid:** A full four-quadrant rainbow, which would fight the single-hue rose palette. Map emotions to tints of rose and plum instead.

## Synthesis for MyEx

1. **Draft Jail has prior art but no premium version.** Break-Up Boss ("Text your Ex", 2017) and ExDetox (Panic Button) both let you write without sending. Neither has a timed lock. A Live Activity countdown (Structured) plus a "let it burn" release (How We Feel) would set MyEx apart.
2. **Use both a strict and a forgiving progress metric.** Keep the strict no-contact streak (Streaks treats it as a "negative habit"). Add a counter that never resets (the Not Boring 60-repetition idea, Let it Go's "resists" count) so a relapse doesn't wipe out all visible progress.
3. **Mira's trust UX.** Always label Mira as AI. Keep "delete conversation" visible. Keep a calm, not chirpy persona. Never paywall part of a reply (Replika's top complaint). Put a crisis line on screen (Ebb, Ash).
4. **Liquid Glass precedent.** How We Feel and Stoic both mention Liquid Glass in release notes. Stoic says "Liquid Glass controls", which matches MyEx's controls-only rule.
5. **Paywall.** Use a short form with two plans, an effective monthly price, social proof first and no trial toggle (RevenueCat case studies). No recurring promotional pop-ups (Reflectly complaints). SOS and crisis tools are never paywalled.
6. **Visual gap.** Every breakup app I could open said nothing about its visual design. None of them publicly claims a premium editorial look. A rose palette with Instrument Serif headlines has no documented direct competitor.
